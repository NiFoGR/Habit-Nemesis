# Habit Nemesis v2: the plan

You are implementing this in the Habit Nemesis repository. Read `CLAUDE.md`
first and obey it: it outranks anything here about comments, copy, the type
scale and the palette.

This document is the whole brief. It says what to build, in what order, in
which files, and how to know each piece is done. Where it names exact copy or
exact hex values, use them verbatim.

**Working rules for every phase**

- Branch: `claude/tender-heisenberg-xvw2l5`. Commit at the end of each phase.
- `npm run check` must pass before every commit. It runs the Arena calendar
  maths and the stylesheet's own rules.
- `npm run dev` and walk every route you touched. No console errors.
- New or renamed file: update `SHELL` in `www/sw.js` and bump `CACHE`.
- No em dashes anywhere, in code, copy, commits or PR text.
- Comments are labels, one line. Reasons go in the PR body.
- Do not add a font size that is not one of the ten rungs in `:root`.
- Do not add a hex outside `:root`. `npm run check:ui` fails on both.

**Effort labels.** P0 ships first and is small. P1 is the differentiating work.
P2 is parity and polish. Do not start a P1 until every P0 is green.

## 1. Where the app stands, and against whom

Four competitors were scouted in full. The findings that drive this plan:

| | Gamified | Competitive | Offline | Account | Ads | Analytics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Habitica | Heavy RPG | Cooperative only | No | Required | No | - |
| HabitNow | None | None | Yes | Optional | Yes | GA + Crashlytics |
| Loop | None | None | Total | None | No | None |
| Finch | Heavy nurture | Refuses by design | No | Required | No | - |
| **Habit Nemesis** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes, and it carries the record** | **Yes** | **None** |

Two empty cells in that grid are the whole product strategy.

**Nobody occupies the competitive column.** Habitica rejects rankings on stated
design philosophy and offers cooperative parties instead. Finch structures its
social layer so only one person's goal is ever visible, explicitly to avoid
comparison. HabitNow and Loop have no gamification at all. The Arena is not a
better version of an existing thing. It is the thing nobody built.

**Nobody is gamified and offline and free of a mandatory account.** Habitica
and Finch buy their gamification with a server. Loop and HabitNow buy their
independence by being spreadsheets.

### What to take from each, and how it gets beaten

| Take | From | How v2 beats it |
|---|---|---|
| Home screen widgets | Loop ships 7, HabitNow ships several | Loop has no match to show. Ours shows the fixture and the score, which no competitor can copy without building an Arena first |
| Inline notification actions | Loop, Yes/No and enter-value on the notification | Ours carries the match state in the text, and cancels itself the instant the cell is marked anywhere |
| Reminders that actually fire | HabitNow's single most repeated complaint is alarms that keep ringing after completion | Fix it, then say so in the store listing. This is a free win against a 7M install incumbent |
| One-off price, no subscription | HabitNow's most praised feature at $11.99 | Match the model, undercut the price, and never gate the Arena or the history |
| Quantity and timed habits | HabitNow, and its checklist type is premium-gated | Ship all three types free, including checklists |
| Curated task bundles | Habitica Challenges | Protocols: a multi-week program that builds the rows and awards its own cup |
| A daily reason to open that is not guilt | Finch's once-a-day free currency | The daily line: one sentence from your Nemesis, once a day, drawn from your own record |
| An async return beat | Finch's 6 to 8 hour adventures | Full time: the day closes at your day-start hour and the result lands as one push |
| A recovery path that does not shame | Finch never punishes, and its retention is the category's best | On Notice: relegation takes two consecutive bad months, and the first one is a visible state, not a silent grace |
| Score charts, streak lists, heatmaps | Loop, best in class | Already mostly built. Add the one thing Loop lacks: an explanation of why the number moved |

### What not to copy, on purpose

- **Habitica's shared punishment.** One member's missed Daily damaging the whole
  party is a documented churn driver. Self versus own history cannot have that
  failure mode. Keep it that way.
- **Finch's total forgiveness.** It is structurally incompatible with this app.
  The edge is that the stakes are fair and earned, not that they are soft.
- **Streak freezes bought with currency.** It cheapens the only number that
  means anything. The mercy rule in Phase 6 is the answer instead.
- **A mandatory account.** The grid must work on first launch with no network.
  See Phase 3 for how the account is sold without becoming a wall.

### The one line the app is sold on

> Your only opponent is the best week you have ever had.

Use this on the first onboarding page, in the Play short description, and on
the first store screenshot. Nowhere else. It stops working if it is everywhere.

## 2. Phase 0: the look, taken from the mark (P0)

**Why.** The icon is two colours and nothing else: `#0a0c10` ground and
`#e62429` red. This was verified by sampling `store/icon-512.png`, where those
two values are 99.6% of all pixels. The app on screen is currently teal with a
violet gradient, so the launcher icon and the app do not look like the same
product. Fix the app, never the mark.

### The palette

Replace the `ground` and `meaning` blocks at the top of `www/styles.css` with
exactly this. Keep the existing comments' style: one line, a label.

```css
  /* ---- ground ---- */
  /* The launcher icon's own black. The ramp above it is 1.16, 1.35, 1.73. */
  --bg: #0a0c10;
  --bg-2: #101419;
  --surface: #171d28;
  --surface-2: #2a3446;
  --line: #3a4459;
  --line-soft: #262e3d;
  --text: #e6eaf0;
  --muted: #97a1b0;
  --faint: #6b7686;

  /* ---- meaning ---- */
  /* The mark's red. The app, the Nemesis, and anything you can act on. */
  --accent: #e62429;
  /* The same red where it has to be read as words. 6.0:1 on the ground. */
  --accent-ink: #ff5257;
  /* A pressed fill, and the groove under a red bar. */
  --accent-deep: #8f1417;
  --on-accent: #ffffff;
  --good: #46d17f;   /* done, ahead of the bar */
  --warn: #f5a524;   /* at risk */
  --ash: #5b6472;    /* missed. A miss is absence, not an alarm */
  --calm: #3aa8f0;   /* skipped, deliberately not counted */
  --danger: #ff5257; /* destructive. The one place the brand red warns */
  --brand: linear-gradient(120deg, #e62429, #8f1417);
```

Delete `--mark` and `--violet`. The mark is now the accent, so a second token
for it is a second source of truth.

**Contrast, already checked, do not re-derive:**

- `#e62429` on `#0a0c10` is 4.32:1. Fills, rings, bars and icons only. Never
  body text.
- `#ff5257` on `#0a0c10` is 6.0:1. This is the red for words.
- `#ffffff` on `#e62429` is 4.53:1. Passes for button labels. Keep them at
  `--w-medium` or heavier.
- `--ash` is 3.3:1. Glyphs and fills only, never text.

### The rule that keeps it one screen

Red is now the app's colour, so **red cannot also mean wrong**. A missed day is
`--ash`, which reads as absence. That is an improvement on its own: the current
grid shouts at you in `--danger` for every day you did not do a thing, which is
exactly the register that makes people delete a habit tracker.

A done cell keeps wearing the habit's own colour. That is the documented
exception and it stays.

### The cut

The mark is an N with a lightning cut through the diagonal. Make that the one
piece of ornament the app owns.

Add to `www/styles.css`:

```css
/* The mark's diagonal, as a rule. One per screen, never two. */
.cut {
  height: 2px;
  border: 0;
  margin: 18px 0;
  background: linear-gradient(90deg, var(--accent), transparent 70%);
  transform: skewX(-12deg);
}
```

Use it above the fixture on the Arena, and above the cups in the Cabinet.
Nowhere else. If a third place wants one, delete one of the first two.

### The clean-up this forces

`npm run check:ui` only reads `www/styles.css`. There are 40 hardcoded hexes in
`www/js` that it cannot see. Find them:

```
grep -rn "#[0-9a-fA-F]\{3,8\}\b" www/js --include=*.js | grep -v vendor
```

Handle them by file:

- `www/js/habits/program.js`, 12 hexes: the habit colour palette. Keep the idea,
  re-cut the values so every one of them sits legibly on the new ground and none
  of them is within touching distance of `#e62429`. Drop the existing red from
  the palette if there is one. A habit's colour must never be mistaken for the
  app's.
- `www/js/arena/crest.js`, 9 hexes: division crest fallbacks. Leave them. Crests
  are the second documented exception.
- `www/js/arena/share.js`, 10 hexes: the share card canvas. Retune to the new
  palette. This image is the app's advertising, so it matters more than any
  screen. Black ground, red accent, white numerals, ash for the opponent.
- `www/js/intro.js`, 6 hexes: `#a78bfa` and the cup tints. Replace with tokens
  read off the palette, or with the new values.
- `www/js/webview.js`, 3 hexes: the unsupported browser page. It ships without
  the stylesheet, so it keeps its own colours. Update them to black and red.

**Done when:** `npm run check:ui` passes, the launcher icon and the running app
read as the same product, no teal or violet remains anywhere in `www/`, and a
missed day is grey rather than red.

## 3. Phase 1: the settings, cut down (P0)

**Why.** `www/js/settings.js` is 330 lines rendering about twenty controls on
one scroll. It opens with a storage-usage hero that answers a question nobody
asked. The user's own verdict is that it is confusing, and they are right.

### The new shape

Settings becomes six rows and a card. Nothing else on the root.

```
[ Account card ]
Grid              >
Alerts            >
Appearance        >
Privacy and lock  >
Your data         >
About             >
```

The account card sits at the top and shows state, not a label:

- Signed in: `Backed up`, then the email in `--muted`, then `Synced 2 minutes
  ago` in `--faint`.
- Signed out: `Not backed up`, then `This record lives on this phone only.`,
  then a `Sign in` button in the accent.

Delete the storage-usage hero entirely. Delete the line
`on this phone. Your record never leaves it.` It is no longer true once the
account is the default, and it was never the selling point the user wanted.

### What goes on each sub-page

| Page | Route | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | `#/settings/grid` | Week starts, a new day begins at, days on screen, oldest first. Then an **Advanced** disclosure holding: toggle with a short press, skip days, question marks for missing data |
| Alerts | `#/settings/alerts` | Notification permission state and the ask button, sound (three states, see Phase 7), vibration, quiet hours |
| Appearance | `#/settings/appearance` | Theme (Dark, Pure black), reduce motion, and later the accent choice if Pro ships |
| Privacy and lock | `#/settings/privacy` | App lock and PIN, ad privacy choices |
| Your data | `#/settings/data` | Archived habits, export backup, import backup, habits as CSV, restore points, erase all data |
| About | `#/settings/about` | Privacy policy, terms, wellbeing, licences, version string, replay the introduction |

**Implementation.** Keep one file, `www/js/settings.js`, and give it a page
parameter rather than splitting into six modules. The repo rule is one folder
per feature and settings is shell, so six files here would be six files at the
top level for one screen. Structure it as a `PAGES` table of
`{ id, title, render }` and one exported `renderSettings(mount, page)`. Register
the routes in `www/js/app.js` and add each to `NAV` so `back.js` answers for
them.

Every sub-page's back arrow is `data-back="settings"`. The root's is
`data-back="hub"`.

**Done when:** the Settings root is one screen with no scrolling on a 6 inch
phone, every control still works, and no setting was deleted, only moved.

## 4. Phase 2: onboarding and the account (P0)

**Why.** Two problems. The intro sells local storage, which the user does not
want sold. And the account exists in full in `www/js/account/` but is never
offered, because `SUPABASE_URL` is empty and `configured()` returns false, so
every account affordance hides itself.

### 4a. Turn the account on

This is configuration, not code. `docs/ACCOUNTS.md` is the complete runbook and
it is accurate. In short:

1. Create the Supabase project in an EU region.
2. Run `supabase/schema.sql` in the SQL editor.
3. Paste the project URL and the publishable key into
   `www/js/account/config.js`.
4. Set up custom SMTP before anyone but you signs up. The built-in sender
   allows two emails an hour for the entire project.
5. Google sign-in needs the OAuth client plus three registered SHA-1
   fingerprints: debug, upload, and the Play App Signing key. Missing the third
   is why Google sign-in works in a sideloaded APK and fails for every store
   install.
6. Turn on the keep-alive workflow, or pay, before submitting. A free project
   pauses after seven days of low activity and a reviewer meeting a paused
   project sees every sign-in fail.

**If the implementing agent has no Supabase credentials**, do not stub them.
Build everything else in this phase, leave `config.js` empty, and note in the
PR body that the account screens are dark until the two values land. The code
already degrades correctly.

### 4b. Sync becomes automatic

Today `www/js/account/sync.js` is a manual backup: `push()`, `pull()`, and a
prompt. That is the wrong default now that the account is the story.

Change it to:

- **Push on change, debounced.** Any store mutation while signed in and online
  schedules a push 5 seconds out. Coalesce. Never push more than once every 30
  seconds.
- **Push on background.** `visibilitychange` to hidden flushes the pending push.
- **Pull on launch.** After `account.init()`, compare `updated_at` to
  `store.lastSynced()`. If the server is ahead and this device has no unsynced
  change, pull silently. That is the new phone case and it should be invisible.
- **Ask only on a real conflict.** Both sides changed since the last sync: show
  the existing choice sheet, once, naming both timestamps.
- **Offline is not an error.** Queue and retry. Never toast a network failure
  during normal use. The record is safe on the device either way.

Keep `store.importJson()` as the only way a server row enters the app, so a row
is sanitised exactly like a file from a stranger. Do not weaken that.

Add a sync state to the store that the Settings card and the Grid nudge read:
`idle`, `pending`, `synced`, `offline`, `error`.

### 4c. The new introduction

`www/js/intro.js` keeps its shape: pages, one picture and one line each, two of
them done rather than read. Change the content and add a page.

**Page 1. The cold open.**
Title: `Habit Nemesis`
Line: `You do not play other people. You play the best week you have ever had.`
Art: the mark at 76, on black.
CTA: `Show me`

Delete the current line about living on your phone.

**Page 2. Mark the day.** Unchanged. It is the best page in the app.

**Page 3. A row is anything.** Unchanged.

**Page 4. The week is a match.** Unchanged copy. Animate the two bars filling
on arrival, 400ms, respecting reduced motion.

**Page 5. The ladder.** Unchanged.

**Page 6. What you keep.** Unchanged.

**Page 7. Start with these.** Unchanged. Starters must be picked before the
account page, so that signing in has something to carry.

**Page 8, new. Keep the record.**
Title: `Keep the record`
Line: `Your account holds the grid, the ladder and the cabinet. New phone, same
record.`
Art: none. Three controls stacked:

```
[ Continue with Google ]   primary, accent fill
[ Continue with email  ]   secondary
  Not now                  text link, --muted, centred
```

`Not now` finishes onboarding exactly as today. It is a link, not a button,
because it is the third choice. It is never disabled and never hidden. A wall
here costs more installs than the sync is worth, and Play's own guidance
expects an account to be optional when the app functions without one.

If the account already holds a record, do not import it silently during
onboarding. Finish onboarding, land on the grid, then show the choice sheet.

**When `configured()` is false**, page 8 is skipped entirely. The build with no
project is still coherent.

### 4d. The nudge, for anyone who chose Not now

One card on the Grid, below the rows, above the review CTA. Not a banner, not a
modal, and it never comes back once dismissed twice.

```
No account
Lose the phone, lose the record.
[ Sign in ]                        [ x ]
```

### 4e. The copy that has to change with it

Search `www/` for every claim that the record never leaves the device and make
each one true again. At minimum:

- `www/js/settings.js`: the hero, deleted in Phase 1.
- `readme.txt`: the opening paragraph already hedges correctly. Re-read it.
- `www/legal/privacy.html`: **this is the one that matters.** Once an account is
  the default path, personal data leaves the device for a processor. The policy
  must name Supabase, the EU region, what is stored (one JSON record and an
  email address), the lawful basis, the retention rule and the deletion route,
  which already exists as `delete_own_account()`. Do not ship the account
  without this. Getting it wrong is a store removal, not a bug.
- The Play Data Safety form: it now declares collection of an email address and
  app activity, in transit encryption, and a deletion route. Update
  `docs/STORE.md` with the answers so the form is filled from a written source
  rather than from memory.

**Done when:** a fresh install can sign in with Google in under 20 seconds,
a second device shows the same grid with no manual step, `Not now` reaches the
grid in one tap, and no screen claims the data stays on the phone.

## 5. Phase 3: the grid, and what it is missing (P1)

The grid is the app and it is already good. Four additions, in order.

### 5a. Perfect day (P0)

Every competitor tracks a streak of days. Habitica sells "Perfect Day" as a
marketing screenshot. The grid already computes `dueToday()`.

When every row owed today is answered and none is a miss, the day header ring
fills solid accent and the `complete` cue plays once. Record the count of
perfect days and surface it in the Cabinet as a feat tier. One line on the
grid, no new screen.

### 5b. The catch-up sheet (P0)

Open the app after missing a day and the honest response is not silence. Show a
sheet listing yesterday's unanswered rows, one tap each, and a `Nothing to
correct` dismiss.

Rules: only the previous day, never a longer backfill from here, the calendar
already does that. Never shown twice for the same day. Never shown before the
first week is complete.

This is the single highest-value quality of life item in the plan. Every
tracker loses users to a two day gap that then feels unrecoverable.

### 5c. Two new habit kinds (P1)

`www/js/habits/edit.js` owns the type picker and `program.js` owns the model.

**Timed.** A habit whose unit is minutes and which can be run rather than typed.
Tapping today's cell starts a full screen timer. The numeral uses `--f-mega`,
which exists for exactly this and is currently unused. Pausing keeps the
partial. Finishing writes the value and plays `timer-done`.

**Checklist.** A habit with up to eight sub-items. The cell shows a partial
ring, done when all are ticked. HabitNow charges for this. Ship it free and put
it in the store listing.

Both must round-trip through `hydrate()` and both must be legible to the Arena's
scoring without special cases: a timed habit is a quantity habit with a unit of
minutes, a checklist habit is a quantity habit with a target of n and a floor.
Model them that way and the Arena needs no change at all.

### 5d. Why the number moved (P1)

Loop's charts are excellent and Loop's users still ask why the score dropped.
Nobody in the category answers it.

On a habit's own screen, under the score, one sentence generated from the
record: `Down 8 points this week. Two misses on Tuesday and Friday, and the
half-life is 13 days.` One line, computed, never stored.

**Done when:** all four ship, `npm run check` passes, and the Arena's scoring
required no new branches for the two new kinds.

## 6. Phase 4: the Arena, sharpened (P1)

The Arena is the USP. Three changes, and one of them is a rule change that needs
the docs and the checker updated with it.

### 6a. On Notice, the mercy rule (P0)

**The problem.** Relegation after one bad month is the app's churn cliff. Finch's
entire retention advantage comes from never putting a user in a state they want
to escape by uninstalling.

**The change.** A month below your division's bar puts you **On Notice**. A
second consecutive month below it relegates you. A month at or above the bar
clears it.

**Why it is not softness.** The state is visible, named, and on the crest. It
raises the tension of the second month rather than removing the stake. It also
creates the best comeback the app can offer, which is a feat.

Files: the month settle in `www/js/arena/ledger.js`, the crest and standing in
`www/js/arena/standing.js` and `crest.js`, the ceremony in `rank.js`. Add the
`Cleared` and `Comeback` feats in `feats.js`.

Update `docs/ARENA.md` in the same commit, and extend `tools/check-arena.mjs`
with cases for: below then above clears, below then below relegates, and a
promotion from an On Notice state clears it.

### 6b. Full time (P0)

Finch's asynchronous adventures create a return beat with no notification spam.
The Arena already has a natural one and does not use it.

At the day-start hour, the previous day closes. Send one local notification:

- Ahead: `Full time. You took the day 5-4.`
- Behind: `Full time. The Nemesis took it 5-3. Six days left.`
- The last day of the week: the week's result instead, which routes to the
  existing result screen.

One a day, at a time the user already chose, off by default until they enable
notifications, and silenced by quiet hours. Use the existing alarm plumbing in
`www/js/native.js` and `arena/program.js`'s `syncAlarms()`.

### 6c. The daily line (P1)

Finch's daily free currency is a Wordle-shaped hook: once a day, costs nothing,
pulls the user in. The equivalent here is not currency, it is voice.

One sentence from the Nemesis at the top of the Arena, once a day, drawn from
the real record. A table of templates filled from live numbers, never random
flattery:

- `You have not missed a Monday since March. Today is Monday.`
- `Your best week was 91%. You are on 64% with three days left.`
- `Four in a row. The Nemesis has never lost five.`
- `Two weeks since you beat me.`

Rules: exactly one a day, never two on screen, never on the grid, always true.
If no template's precondition holds, show nothing rather than filler.

This is the cheapest retention mechanic in the plan and the most on-brand. Build
it as a predicate table in the same shape as `feats.js`, which is already the
one catalogue of "things that are true about the record".

**Done when:** a month below the bar shows On Notice rather than relegating,
`npm run check:arena` covers the new rule, full time fires once, and the daily
line never repeats within a day and is never false.

## 7. Phase 5: widgets and notifications (P1, and the largest piece)

**Why it is first among the P1s.** Loop ships seven widget types. HabitNow ships
several with tap to check. Habit Nemesis ships none, and `docs/BRAINSTORM.md`
already names this as the one thing the source app does that this cannot. It is
also the only surface where the Arena can advertise itself on a home screen.

### The three widgets

1. **Grid.** Rows by days, exactly the app's grid, tap a cell to mark. 4x2 and
   4x4.
2. **Fixture.** You and the Nemesis, two numbers and one track, the crest in the
   corner. 4x2. This is the one no competitor can build.
3. **Today.** A single ring, how much of today is answered, and the count still
   owed. 2x2.

### The architecture, because Capacitor has no widget API

The web app cannot draw a widget. The widget cannot read localStorage. So:

1. On every mutation, `store.js` writes a compact snapshot through a new
   Capacitor plugin into Android `SharedPreferences`: rows, the last seven days
   of marks, this week's two scores, the crest id. Keep it under 8KB.
2. The widget is native, under `native/widgets/`, following the pattern
   `native/systemui/` already establishes. A plugin package, never a patch
   script, because `android/` is regenerated on every build.
3. Draw with Glance if the Compose dependency is acceptable, otherwise
   RemoteViews. RemoteViews is the safer choice for minSdk 24.
4. A tap sends a broadcast the plugin receives. It writes the mark into the
   snapshot and into a pending-changes queue. The web app drains that queue on
   next resume and reconciles.
5. The widget re-renders on snapshot write, not on a timer.

Budget this as the largest single task in the plan. If it has to slip, ship the
Fixture widget alone. One widget nobody else has beats three that Loop already
has.

### Notifications, and the incumbent's open wound

HabitNow's most repeated one and two star complaint is alarms that keep firing
after a habit is completed or skipped, and the developer's answer is to blame
the device's battery optimiser. This is a gift.

Rules for v2:

- Marking a cell cancels that habit's pending alarm for the day, from every
  surface: app, widget, notification action.
- Notification actions: `Done` and `Skip` on a boolean habit, `Enter` on a
  quantity habit, mirroring Loop's model.
- The reminder text carries the match: `Run. The Nemesis is one ahead.`
- Never more than one Arena notification a day.
- Quiet hours, set in Alerts, suppress everything except an alarm-mode reminder
  the user explicitly set.
- On Android 12 and up there is no notification-level snooze, which is a
  platform restriction and not worth fighting. Do not build a workaround.

**Done when:** marking anywhere cancels the alarm everywhere, and a fresh
install with one habit and one reminder gets exactly one notification.

## 8. Phase 6: quality of life (P1 and P2)

Ordered by value. Ship top down and stop when the phase's time is gone.

1. **Undo instead of confirm.** Deleting a habit, clearing a day, restoring a
   snapshot: act immediately, show a toast with `Undo` for 6 seconds. Keep the
   typed confirm for `Erase all data` alone. Modal confirms are the slowest
   thing in the app.
2. **Search.** One field above the grid when there are more than 12 rows.
   Matches name and note. Loop shipped this only recently and HabitNow lacks it.
3. **Duplicate a habit.** One menu item. Cheap, and asked for in every tracker's
   reviews.
4. **A note on a day, not only on a habit.** Already the top item in
   `docs/BRAINSTORM.md`'s backlog. Long press a past cell, add a line. Surfaced
   in the week review.
5. **Protocols (P1, and it is a feature not a nicety).** A curated multi-week
   program that creates its rows, runs for a fixed span, and awards its own
   entry in the Cabinet. Ship three: a 30 day discipline block, a lifting split,
   a sleep protocol. This is Habitica's Challenges without needing a server or
   another person. Store the definition as data in `habits/program.js`, never as
   a screen full of special cases.
6. **App shortcuts.** Long press the launcher icon: `Mark today`, `Arena`.
7. **Pure black theme.** Loop's `pref_pure_black` exists for AMOLED and its users
   love it. `--bg: #000000` and one class on `<html>`.
8. **Habit icons.** 24 of them, not HabitNow's 130. A dot with a glyph reads
   faster than a dot.
9. **Repair and report.** Loop ships "repair database" and "generate bug
   report". The equivalent here is a diagnostics line in About that copies the
   store version, the schema version and the last sync state to the clipboard.

## 9. Phase 7: sound and haptics (P1)

`www/js/ui.js` already has a real synth: one context, a compressor, a shared
key, graded peaks. It is better than anything a competitor ships. Extend it,
do not replace it.

### The setting changes shape

`Sound` becomes three states, not a toggle:

| State | Master gain | Plays |
|---|---|---|
| Off | 0 | nothing |
| Subtle | 1.0 | marks, unmarks, skips only |
| Full | 1.7 | everything, including the ceremonies |

Default to Full on a new install. Store as a string, migrate the old boolean in
`hydrate()`: `true` becomes `full`, `false` becomes `off`.

Add quiet hours to Alerts: between 22:00 and 07:00 the app plays nothing above
Subtle, whatever the setting says.

### New cues

Stay in the existing key and use the existing `N` note table. Peaks stay graded:
a cell mark is a whisper, a cup is the loudest thing the app does.

| Cue | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| `kickoff` | The week's first mark, Monday | `g` then `C`, short, peak 0.10 |
| `fulltime` | The day closes | single `c`, 0.3s, peak 0.08, no strike |
| `overtake` | You pass the Nemesis mid-week | `e`, `G`, `C2` rising, bright 6, peak 0.13. Once a week |
| `behind` | The Nemesis passes you | single `d`, 0.35s, glide 0.94, peak 0.08. Once a day |
| `notice` | The month settles On Notice | `g` falling with glide, 0.5s, no strike |
| `timer-tick` | Final ten seconds of a timed habit | `A`, 0.03s, peak 0.02 |
| `timer-done` | A timed habit completes | strike at 1600 plus `C` and `G` |
| `perfect` | Every row owed today is answered | the `complete` cue plus a `C2` tail at 0.5s |

`relegate` keeps having no strike. A punishing noise is how you get someone to
stop opening the app, and that reasoning is already written in the file.

### Haptics

Extend `PATTERNS` with `kickoff: [0, 25, 40, 25]`, `fulltime: 20`,
`overtake: [0, 30, 40, 30, 40, 60]`, `notice: [0, 90, 60, 90]`.

Every new cue gets a haptic sibling. Sound and vibration are one language and a
cue that only exists in one of them feels broken to anyone who runs silent.

## 11. The money, restated

`docs/RELEASE.md` milestone 3 sets the free and paid line. One change to it,
driven by the scouting:

HabitNow's most praised single attribute across every source is a **one-off
$11.99 unlock and no subscription**. Finch's most attacked single attribute is
its **billing**: early paywall, auto-converting trial, price creep, unexplained
platform price gap. Habitica charges $4.99 a month.

So: **one payment, no subscription, and undercut HabitNow.** Around £6.99.

Never gated: the grid, unlimited habits, the Arena, every division, the cups,
the feats, the whole history, export, and the PIN.

Behind the one payment: no ads, the widgets, extra themes, and the share card
without a watermark.

Say `No subscription, ever.` on the listing and in the paywall. Against three
competitors who all charge monthly, that sentence is worth more than the
feature list above it.
