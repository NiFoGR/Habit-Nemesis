Habit Nemesis

Health and wellbeing

Last updated [NOT SET: effective].

This app counts things you have chosen to count, and scores you against your own past. That is useful for a lot of people and it is not right for everyone. This page says what the app is, what it is not, and where to go if it stops helping.

1What this app is not

It is not a medical device. It is not registered with the MHRA and it has not been assessed by anyone as a health product.

It gives no medical, psychological, nutritional or fitness advice. It does not know what you should be doing, and it never suggests a habit, a target or a number to you. Every row on the grid is one you wrote yourself.

Nothing it shows you is a clinical measurement, and no score on any screen means anything about your health.

2What a score actually is

A record of what you chose to mark, and nothing more.

The percentage on a habit is an average of the days you marked, weighted so recent days count more. Your division is that average over a month, compared to a fixed number. Your opponent each week is a week out of your own history.

It cannot tell whether you did the thing. It only knows what you tapped. That matters because a low score is not evidence about you: it is evidence about a screen you did not open, which is often a different thing entirely.

3When to talk to a person instead

A habit tracker is a poor place to work some things out, and it is worth knowing which.

Please speak to your GP, or one of the services in section 4, if any of this sounds like you:

  • You are tracking food, weight, calories or exercise and the tracking has started to feel like the point rather than a tool.
  • Missing a day makes you anxious, or you exercise or restrict to make up for a missed one.
  • You are keeping a streak going when you are ill, injured or exhausted.
  • The number on this app affects how you feel about yourself.
  • You are having thoughts of harming yourself, or you feel you cannot go on.

The last one is not a wellbeing matter to think about later. Call 999, or Samaritans on 116 123 now.

5Turning the pressure down

Streaks, divisions and cups exist to make keeping something going feel worth doing. That works right up until it does not, and there is no virtue in pushing through a feature that has stopped helping you.

Everything here is reversible, and none of it costs you your record:

IfDo this
One habit has become a stick to hit yourself with Open the habit, tap the pencil, then Archive. It leaves the grid and stops being scored. Its history is kept and Settings can bring it back.
You want a day off without breaking anything Turn on Skip days in Settings. Tapping a cell twice marks it skipped, which holds the score and the streak exactly where they are.
Missing days feels worse than it should Turn off Question marks for missing data in Settings, so a day you never answered stops being drawn as a failure.
The competition is the problem You never have to open the Arena. The grid works on its own, and nothing on it depends on a division.
You want to stop entirely Delete the app. If you want the record first, Settings › Export backup writes a file. Erase all data clears everything on the phone.

6Nobody else sees any of this

There is no feed, no leaderboard against other people, and no sharing that you did not start yourself. Your only opponent is a week out of your own record.

Nothing is shared with anyone by default. The share card exists, and it only ever goes where you send it. The privacy policy has the full picture of what is stored and where.
