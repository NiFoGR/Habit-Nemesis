2What the app holds today

Active now

Everything below is written by the app into your device's own storage, under two keys: habitnemesis.state.v1 for the record, and habitnemesis.snap.YYYY-MM-DD for the daily restore points. Nothing is sent anywhere. The list is taken from the code that defines the record, not from a template.

Your habits

  • The habit's name, its question, and your free-text notes on it.
  • Its colour, whether it is a yes/no habit or a number, its unit, its target and whether the target is a floor or a ceiling.
  • How often you want to do it, written as a number of times in a number of days.
  • The group you filed it in, and that group's name.
  • The time of day and the weekdays you asked to be reminded.
  • Whether it is archived, when it was archived, when it was created, and when it was last edited.

The days you marked

For each habit, one entry per day: the date, and what you recorded. A skip, a lapse, or the number you logged. This is the record itself, and it is the whole point of the app.

The Arena's record

  • Every closed week: your score, days done, days due, the opponent week, its name and score, whether you won, lost or voided it, the cup round it belonged to, and any note you wrote on it.
  • Every month: your score, wins, losses, the division you started and finished in, and whether you moved.
  • Every season arc: whether you qualified and how far you got, plus any note.
  • Your current division, the feats you have earned and when, and which result and ceremony screens you have already seen.

Your settings

Haptics, sound, first day of the week, the hour a day starts, tap or press-and-hold to mark, whether skip days and unknown days are shown, day order, the number of grid columns, and whether you have seen the introduction. Also the date this device last wrote to or read from an account, which stays on this device and never travels with the record.

The PIN's check blob

If you set an app PIN, the app stores three values: a random salt, a random initialisation vector, and one short encrypted block. Your PIN is not stored, not in plain form and not as a hash of its own. See section 12.

The face photo

You may attach a photo of yourself to your Nemesis, so the best week you ever had has a face. The app crops it square, shrinks it to 256 pixels and saves it as a small JPEG inside the record on your device, along with the week it belongs to and the moment you took it.

The photo is a picture, not a biometric. The app never measures a face, never matches one and never runs recognition of any kind. It draws the image on screen and nothing else. The share card deliberately leaves it out, so sending someone your week never sends them your face.

What is not collected

No name, no email, no phone number, no contacts, no location, no device identifier, no advertising ID, no analytics, no crash reports, no usage statistics. The app has no account today and asks you for nothing about yourself.

4The claim that nothing leaves your phone

The app has said that nothing leaves your phone. That is worth being exact about, because it is true in most places and not true in one.

Where it holds

  • The app makes no network request of its own. There is no server to call and no account configured in this build.
  • The offline cache stores only the app's own files, from the app itself. It fetches nothing from anyone else.
  • There is no analytics, no tracker, no advertising code and no third-party SDK reporting anything.
  • Reminders are scheduled by your phone's own alarm clock. They never reach the internet.
  • The publisher cannot see your record. There is nowhere for it to be seen.

Where it does not hold

Android backup

On Android the app turns on Android's own Auto Backup. Your record is copied to your Google account. That is a real copy of your habits, your marked days, the Arena's history, your face photo and your PIN's check blob, held by Google. Section 7 covers what that means and how to turn it off.

Two other things leave the device, but only when you send them:

  • The share card. If you make one and hand it to the share sheet, it goes wherever you send it. It never includes your face photo.
  • Your export. The JSON backup and the CSV are files you save. Once saved they are ordinary files on your device and are as private as wherever you put them.

When accounts, sync and ads arrive, more will leave. Section 3 says exactly what.

7Android Auto Backup

On Android, this app deliberately turns on Android's Auto Backup, and the whole record is included.

Everything the app stores lives inside its own private folder on the phone, and Android deletes that folder when you uninstall the app. Auto Backup is the only way that record survives a new phone without a server, so the app switches it on.

What is copied. The app's stored data: your habits, your marked days, the Arena's history, your settings, your face photo, your restore points and the PIN's check blob. Your PIN itself is not in there, because it is never stored anywhere.

Where it goes. Your own Google account, not the publisher's. Google also uses the same data for device-to-device transfer when you set up a new phone. The publisher has no access to it and cannot read it, delete it or ask for it.

How it is protected. Google encrypts these backups. On devices with a screen lock, Google encrypts them with a key tied to that lock, which means Google itself cannot read them. The app does not control that and does not require it, so on a device that cannot provide it the backup still goes ahead with Google's server-side encryption alone. If that matters to you, turn the backup off.

How to turn it off. Android Settings, then Google, then Backup. You can switch backup off for this app, and you can delete an existing backup from the same place. Turning it off means an uninstall loses your record, so export a copy from the app's Settings first.

Google's role here. This is a service Google provides to you under your Google account. The publisher is not a party to it and does not receive the data.

Deletion is deletion, not deactivation, and it is not reversible. The record cannot be recovered afterwards. Export a copy first if you want one.

12The PIN, and what losing it costs

The app lock works like this. A random salt is generated on your device. Your PIN and that salt are stretched through 250,000 rounds of PBKDF2 into an encryption key, and that key encrypts one short known phrase. Only the salt, the initialisation vector and the encrypted phrase are stored. When you enter a PIN, the app derives a key from it and tries to decrypt that phrase. The right PIN works. The wrong one fails and reveals nothing.

There is no recovery

Your PIN is not stored anywhere, so it cannot be looked up, reset or bypassed. Not by you, not by the publisher, not by anyone with the phone in their hand. If you forget it, the only way back into the app is to erase its data and start again, and your entire record goes with it.

This is what makes the lock worth having, and it is the price. The app warns you before you set one. Export a backup from Settings and keep it somewhere safe, and a forgotten PIN costs you an import rather than everything.

The lock protects the app's screens. It is not full-disk encryption: the record itself is stored unencrypted in the app's private folder, where your phone's own encryption and screen lock protect it. The PIN is not copied into an export and is not carried in by an import, because it belongs to the device rather than to the record.

14Local storage, cookies and PECR

There are no cookies. There is no analytics, no tracking pixel, no fingerprinting and no advertising identifier in the app today.

The app does store information on your device, in your browser or WebView's local storage, under the two keys named in section 2. The Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations require your consent for storing information on your device, unless that storage is strictly necessary to provide the service you asked for.

Here it is strictly necessary. The stored record is the service. A habit tracker that cannot remember which days you marked is not a habit tracker, and there is no version of this app that works without it. So no consent banner is shown, and showing one would be theatre.

The app's offline cache is in the same position. It stores copies of the app's own files so the app opens without a connection, which is what you installed it for.

Not yet active Advertising is different. An ad SDK's storage and identifiers are not strictly necessary, so they need consent. Before any advertising code runs, a Google-certified consent platform will ask you, and nothing ad-related is stored on your device until you have answered. You can change that answer in Settings whenever you like.
